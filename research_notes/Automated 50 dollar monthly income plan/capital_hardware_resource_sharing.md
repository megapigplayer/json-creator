## 1. Cash yield (HYSA, money market funds, T-bills; US plus eurozone/UK/Israel): current rates, Fed path, net $/month per $1,000, and capital needed for $50/month

### Takeaway
Government-backed cash is the only method in this category that delivers a near-certain, fully automated $50/month, but it takes capital. In the US at late-Sept-2026 rates (T-bills ~4.2–4.3%, best no-strings HYSAs ~4.1–4.2%, government money market funds ~3.75% and rising), each $1,000 earns about $3.10–3.60/month before tax. Netting $50/month therefore needs about $14k–16k before tax, or about $18k–21k after a 22% federal bracket. The Fed raised rates on 16 Sep 2026 and markets expect more hikes, so the near-term trend in US yields is up. Eurozone rates are lower (ECB deposit rate 2.50%), UK rates are similar to US (Bank Rate 3.75%, best easy-access accounts 4.5–5.0% including bonuses), and Israeli rates are falling (Bank of Israel 3.25%).

## 2. Dividend/bond ETFs, stablecoin yield, crypto staking, securities lending: yields, net $/month per $1,000, and risks

### Takeaway
None of these beats government cash on certainty for a consistent $50/month:
- **Dividend ETFs** (SCHD ~3.2%) need more capital, pay quarterly and carry equity drawdown risk.
- **Coinbase's USDC rewards** (about 3.5%) now need a $4.99/month Coinbase One subscription, are US-centric, lack FDIC coverage and face a legislative ban on passive yield. They are strictly worse than a 4.2% HYSA.
- **ETH staking** (~3%) and **SOL staking** (~6–7%) pay in volatile tokens, so income and principal are both unstable.
- **Securities lending** (IBKR SYEP pays 50% of the lending rate) earns little on ordinary ETFs.

### Inferences
- **SCHD:** $2.64/month per $1,000 before tax. Averaging $50/month takes about $18.9k pre-tax, or about $22.3k at a 15% dividend tax. It pays quarterly (about $150 a quarter), so it is not monthly-consistent. An equity bear market can cut principal by 20–50% and can cut dividends. Verdict for this goal: **MAYBE**, only for long-horizon investors. It is less certain than T-bills over 3–6 months.
- **VYM:** $1.88/month per $1,000, so about $26.7k is needed pre-tax. Dominated by SCHD and by cash for this goal.
- **Bond ETFs** (no Sept 2026 SEC-yield data found):
  - Short-Treasury ETFs (e.g., SGOV) should track T-bill yields minus fees. They can stand in for T-bills, especially for non-US investors who buy through a broker.
  - Intermediate funds (BND/AGG) lose principal when rates rise, and the Fed is hiking now, so avoid them for this goal.
- **Coinbase USDC** (3.50% with a $4.99/month fee):
  - Needs about $18.9k pre-tax, or about $24.2k at a 22% bracket, for $50/month net of the fee.
  - A 4.20% FDIC-insured HYSA needs only about $18.3k.
  - It is dominated, not FDIC-insured, and US-centric, and a pending bill targets passive yield. Promotions like 6.5% in BTC add BTC price risk. **ELIMINATE.**
- **Staking:**
  - At 3% ETH, $50/month needs about $20k pre-tax ($25.6k at a 22% bracket). At 6.7% SOL it needs about $9k pre-tax ($11.5k at 22%).

  - **ELIMINATE** as an income method.
- **SYEP:** broad ETFs and large caps are cheap to borrow, so lending income on them is usually close to zero. Meaningful lending fees come only from hard-to-borrow, volatile small caps, which carry large principal risk. Treat it as a free add-on toggle, not a $50 source (**ELIMINATE standalone**).

## 3. Bandwidth-sharing ("proxyware") apps: realistic earnings per device/IP, thresholds, datacenter-IP rules, and risks

### Takeaway
Proxyware pays roughly $1–10/month per home IP in 2026 (per-GB rates have fallen to about $0.10–0.20). Earnings scale with unique *residential IPs*, not devices, so $50/month would need about 5–50 separate home connections. That is impossible within ToS, because datacenter/VPS IPs are banned or violate cloud terms. Pawns.app stopped paying for bandwidth in Jan 2026, and PacketStream can take about a year to reach a $5 payout. In both Jan 2026 and Jul 2026, Google explicitly warned consumers to be "extremely wary" of apps that pay for "unused bandwidth". **All proxyware: ELIMINATE for this goal.**

## 6. Security, legal, tax, and ISP-ToS risks, with documented incidents

### Takeaway
Proxyware carries the heaviest documented risk. In 2026 Google twice disrupted large residential-proxy networks (IPIDEA in January, used by 550+ threat groups in one week; NetNut in July, about 2M devices and 316 threat clusters in one June week). Both times Google told consumers to be "extremely wary" of pay-for-bandwidth apps, because traffic through an exit node can reach other devices on the home network and gets the user's IP flagged or blocked. Many major ISPs' acceptable-use policies prohibit reselling bandwidth. Earlier incidents: Cisco Talos (2021) and Sysdig (2023) documented malware and "proxyjacking" campaigns that monetize victims through Honeygain, IPRoyal, Peer2Profit and similar services. Cash yield carries rate risk only; crypto carries principal and regulatory risk; GPU and storage sharing carry electricity, hardware-wear and platform-rate-cut risk.

### Cited Findings
**Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG), 28 Jan 2026 — IPIDEA** [Official, fetched in full]
- IPIDEA is "one of the largest residential proxy networks in the world". Its brands include 922 Proxy, ABC Proxy, Luna Proxy, PIA S5 Proxy, IP 2 World and others.
- It enrolled devices through four SDKs (Castar, Earn, Hex, Packet) embedded in apps. Developers were paid to include them.
- "In a single seven day period in January 2026, GTIG observed over 550 individual threat groups … utilizing IP addresses tracked as IPIDEA exit nodes", including groups from China, the DPRK, Iran and Russia. Activities included "access to victim SaaS environments, on-premises infrastructure, and password spray attacks."
- "IPIDEA proxy did not solely route traffic through the exit node device, they also sent traffic to the device, in order to compromise it."
- "Consumers should be extremely wary of applications that offer payment in exchange for 'unused bandwidth' or 'sharing your internet.' These applications are primary ways for illicit proxy networks to grow, and could open security vulnerabilities on the device's home network."
- Users "provide their IP address and device as a launchpad for hacking … potentially causing them to be flagged as suspicious or blocked by providers."
- "While many residential proxy providers state that they source their IP addresses ethically, our analysis shows these claims are often incorrect or overstated."
- The disruption reduced the device pool "by millions".

— [Google Cloud / GTIG, 28 Jan 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/disrupting-largest-residential-proxy-network)
- Coverage: 600+ trojanized Android apps and 3,000+ trojanized Windows binaries posing as OneDriveSync or Windows Update; IPIDEA advertised itself as a VPN with 6.7M users — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-disrupts-ipidea-residential-proxy-networks-fueled-by-malware/); [Help Net Security, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/01/29/ipidea-proxy-network-disrupted/); [The Hacker News, Jan 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/01/google-disrupts-ipidea-one-of-worlds.html) [Secondary]. **GTIG, 2 Jul 2026 — NetNut ("Popa")** [Official, fetched in full]
- NetNut is estimated at about **2 million devices**. It distributes SDKs for smart TVs and streaming boxes, and includes plugin components for Badbox 2.0.
- "In a single week during June 2026, GTIG observed 316 distinct threat clusters using suspected NetNut exit nodes."

- "Users can have their legitimate traffic flagged as suspicious, or blocked by their service providers."
- Public reports document NetNut being used to spread Mirai variants.
- Play Protect "automatically warned users and disabled applications known to incorporate NetNut SDKs."
- Google again warned consumers to be "extremely wary" of apps paying for "unused bandwidth".

— [Google Cloud / GTIG, 2 Jul 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/google-continued-disruption-residential-proxy-networks)

### Inferences
- **Legal and ToS:** under the assignment's rule that everything must stay within ISP and cloud terms, proxyware is *presumptively* non-compliant on most major consumer ISPs, and datacenter or VPS use is non-compliant with the platforms themselves (Honeygain). Hence **ELIMINATE**. A user whose ISP explicitly allows it would still face Google's documented security and IP-reputation risks for about $2–10/month.
- **Liability:** as a proxy exit node (Mysterium, Honeygain, EarnApp and others), your home IP originates strangers' traffic. The GTIG documents show abuse such as password spraying and SaaS intrusion running over residential exit nodes. Abuse complaints, CAPTCHAs and blocks then land on the user's IP. This is the main non-financial risk.
- **Principal risk ranking** (lowest to highest): T-bills and insured deposits, then government MMFs, then short-Treasury ETFs, then dividend ETFs, then stablecoins (depeg or custodial risk, not insured), then ETH/SOL staking.
- **Tax:** all income here is taxable in most jurisdictions. Interest, MMF dividends, staking rewards and platform payouts (including gift cards) are generally ordinary income. Qualified equity dividends may get lower rates. Treat after-tax figures in Sections 1, 2 and 9 as illustrations at assumed brackets.

- **Stablecoin rewards:** dominated by HYSA; paywalled; regulatory ban risk.

- **Securities lending standalone:** income is about zero on normal holdings.
- **Midrange-GPU compute:** electricity is at or above revenue.
