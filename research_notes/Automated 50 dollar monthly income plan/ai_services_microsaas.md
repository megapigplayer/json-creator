When a summary merged several results, all of them are cited, and the exact attribution is uncertain. I did not read the full pages. ## 1. How likely is a new AI micro-product to reach $50/month NET within 3–6 months, and what are the failure rates?

### Takeaway
The bottleneck is getting customers, not building the product. Roughly half of even Stripe-connected indie products earn $0. The ones that earn anything usually clear $50/month (median revenue for revenue-positive solo products on TrustMRR is about $145–$445 MRR). For a single new AI micro-product with almost no ongoing human marketing, I estimate about a **3–8% chance of ≥$50/month net by month 3 and 8–15% by month 6**. A small portfolio of 3–5 cheap tools on one site raises that to about **8–15% and 15–30%** (all estimates).

### Cited Findings
- An analysis of 937 Stripe-verified products listed on Indie Hackers found **54% generate exactly $0**. The same 54% rate appeared in 2023 and 2024. The top 5% of founders capture 70%+ of revenue. — [Solo Operator Stack](https://solooperatorstack.com/blog/indie-hacker-revenue-distribution-tam-clarity/) (secondary analysis; methodology not verified)
- About 70% of independent SaaS products earn under $500/month, ~18% reach the $1K–$5K band, and only 1–2% clear $50K MRR. The median micro-SaaS earns about $500/month MRR across 1,000+ products studied. — [SaaSRanger](https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/); [BoringRiches](https://boringriches.com/guides/saas-business-revenue-benchmarks/) (the search summary merged both; which figure comes from which is uncertain)
- TrustMRR-based analyses:
  - Median MRR of **$445 across 528 projects with positive revenue**, out of an article titled "What 986 Solo Startups Actually Look Like".
  - **~27% of those earn under $100/month**. Examples: Supaframe at $88 MRR and JobBoardSearch at $99 MRR.
  - More than half earn under $500, and only 13% crossed $5,000 MRR. — [One Million Goal](https://www.onemilliongoal.com/p/what-986-solo-startups-actually-look)
  - Other analyses report a median MRR of $334, and a median of $145 with a mean of ~$4,300. — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr); [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/startup-reality-the-hard-numbers-from-trustmrr) (attribution between these two uncertain)
- TrustMRR verifies revenue through Stripe, RevenueCat, Superwall and Creem connections, with data updated hourly. — [TrustMRR AI category](https://trustmrr.com/category/ai). It lists 2,005 US startups. — [TrustMRR US](https://trustmrr.com/country/us). A third party describes it as holding "8,000+ SaaS startups". — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr). It also lists startups with $0 revenue in the last 30 days and $0 MRR. — [TrustMRR SaaS](https://trustmrr.com/category/saas)
- Tiny verified AI products in or near the target range:
  - My Photo AI, Inc.: **$60 in the last 30 days**. — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/my-photo-ai-inc)
  - "AI Photo Headshot Generator" (Türkiye, founded Nov 2025): **$1.7k revenue in 30 days**, verified via RevenueCat, so it is a mobile app. — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-photo-headshot-generator)
- Build-in-public timelines:
  - Habit Pixel went from $0 to $1K MRR in **8 months** as a solo developer. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-0-to-1k-mrr-in-8-months-bootstrapping-habit-pixel-as-a-solo-dev-684b6c056d)
  - Photo AI (Pieter Levels) went from $0 to $132K MRR in 18 months. — [Indie Hackers case study](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/photo-ai-by-pieter-levels-complete-deep-dive-case-study-0-to-132k-mrr-in-18-months-3a9a2b1579). This is an outlier driven by a large existing audience, so it reflects survivorship bias.
  - One indie hacker reached $6,772 MRR "with just $20/day Meta ads". — [MRR Story](https://www.mrrstory.com/stories/this-indie-hacker-reached-6772-mrr-with-just-20day-meta-ads)
- **Hype to discount.** Search summaries also surfaced claims that indie hackers reach "$1,000–$10,000 MRR within 3–6 months" for under $200. These came from course- or guide-style content such as [IDLEN](https://www.idlen.io/blog/indie-hacker-ai-profitable-projects-no-funding/) and a [Gumroad-sold guide](https://thecodekaizen.gumroad.com/l/YourCompleteGuidetoBuildingProfitableSideProjectswithAIin2026). They are unverified, and which of these pages made the claim is uncertain.

### Inferences
- **Selection bias makes the real base rate worse.** The 54% $0 figure only covers products whose founders bothered to connect Stripe to Indie Hackers. TrustMRR only covers people who chose to list. Products abandoned before taking a payment, or never listed, are invisible. So the true share of launched micro-products that make $0 is likely **well above 54%**.
- **Revenue-positive products usually clear $50/month.** If ~27% of revenue-positive TrustMRR solo projects are under $100/month, then most of them are above $50–$100. So the real hurdle is getting *any* paying customers without human marketing.
- **Rough consistency check (calc, unverified).** If the 528 revenue-positive projects are a subset of the 986 solo startups, then ~46% had no positive revenue. That matches the ~54% $0 figure from Indie Hackers.
- **Time.** Founders who actively build in public took about 8 months to reach $1K MRR (Habit Pixel). A zero-marketing, SEO-plus-directories approach should be slower. SEO on a new domain rarely contributes much before month 3–6 (estimate; no SEO-age data gathered). Month-3 odds therefore rest mostly on one-off launch spikes (directories, Product Hunt).
- **Probability estimates (judgement):**
  - Single tool: 3–8% by month 3, 8–15% by month 6.
  - Portfolio of 3–5 tools on one domain sharing one credit system: 8–15% by month 3, 15–30% by month 6. More search surface and more directory listings help. Failure modes are correlated (same new domain, same builder, same channels), which keeps the odds well below 50%.
- **Survivorship warning.** Nearly all "AI SaaS in a weekend" success stories involve either an existing audience (Photo AI) or ongoing ad management (the $20/day Meta ads story). Neither is available under the user's zero-touch constraint.

### Gaps
- No dataset isolates *new AI micro-products with no human marketing* or measures time-to-first-$50. The probabilities above are judgement anchored to the distributions cited.
- I could not verify the methodologies behind the 54%-zero figure or the TrustMRR analyses (fetch blocked). Their samples, dates and whether $0 products are included are unconfirmed.
- I found no reliable failure-rate data outside self-selected directories such as Indie Hackers and TrustMRR.

## 2. Which niches show validated demand in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Verified-revenue evidence is strongest for **AI photo/headshot products**, with multiple TrustMRR-verified revenues from $60/30 days up to $31k/30 days. That niche is also the most crowded, ad-driven and payment-processor-restricted. I found **no verified revenue data** in this session for AI songs, kids' storybooks, pet portraits, SEO audits, subtitle translation or resume tailoring. Their demand must be validated before building.

### Cited Findings
- **AI headshots, market size.** A vendor puts the AI portrait/headshot segment at **$350–500M in 2025**, up from ~$200M in 2024. — [BetterPic](https://www.betterpic.io/blog/ai-headshot-generator-market-size-research) (vendor estimate, self-interested)
- **AI headshots, competition.** Competitors include HeadshotPro, Aragon AI, Dreamwave, BetterPic and "dozens of white-label clones". HeadshotPro has pivoted to B2B. Prices run from $27 (AI SuitUp) to $99 (Dreamwave premium), and most start around $29–$35. — [Headshots.com](https://www.headshots.com/blog/headshotpro-alternatives/); [BetterPic](https://www.betterpic.io/blog/ai-headshot-generator-market-size-research)
- **AI headshots, verified revenues on TrustMRR:**
  - "AI-generated professional headshots from selfies": $31k revenue in 30 days, $37k MRR, $661k total. — [TrustMRR AI category](https://trustmrr.com/category/ai)
  - AI Photo Headshot Generator: $1.7k in 30 days. — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-photo-headshot-generator)
  - My Photo AI: $60 in 30 days, priced at $15/$35/$65 one-time. — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/my-photo-ai-inc)
  - Looktara claims $22,000 revenue in 150 days and 15k+ users. This is a self-description on its listing, and the specific listing URL was not captured. — [TrustMRR open feed](https://trustmrr.com/open)
- **AI songs.** Suno outputs carry commercial-use rights only when made under a Pro or Premier subscription; free-tier outputs cannot be used commercially.
  - From **September 3, 2026**, commercial exploitation is allowed only for outputs obtained through a "permitted download" within the tier's download allocation. Stream ripping is prohibited.
  - Suno has **no self-serve public API**. In July 2026 it said it was exploring a developer API with a curated partner group. Services selling a "Suno API" "operate independently".
  - Sources: [Suno ToS](https://suno.com/terms-of-service); [GPTProto](https://gptproto.com/blog/suno-api); [Music in Africa (Warner Music partnership changes)](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/suno-adjusts-ai-music-ownership-terms-after-warner-music-partnership/); [Terms.law](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/suno/)
- **Etsy AI items.** Etsy allows sellers to "use their original prompts in combination with AI tools" to create artwork they sell. — [Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004)
- TrustMRR has browsable "AI" and "Startups using OpenAI" categories that could be used for demand validation. — [TrustMRR AI](https://trustmrr.com/category/ai); [TrustMRR OpenAI](https://trustmrr.com/tech/openai). No aggregate numbers were captured.

### Inferences
- The headshot evidence shows people pay **$15–$99 one-time** for a concrete AI deliverable. That is useful pricing guidance: higher one-time prices mean far fewer sales are needed to reach $50 (see §4).
- Headshots are a poor fit for this user even though demand is proven. The niche is saturated with well-funded players, and processors restrict AI images of real people (§5). It also involves face data and refund risk over likeness (§6).
- Text-output niches (resume and cover-letter tailoring, subtitle translation, niche document generators) have negligible model cost and clear MoR acceptance (§4–5). Their weakness is that general chatbots do many of these jobs for free, so a product must add convenience or file handling (e.g., a correctly timed .srt file, a formatted PDF, a job-specific template) that a chat window does not.
- Custom songs built on Suno are effectively blocked by Suno's terms. A version built on a licensed music API with commercial rights might work, but I could not verify any.
- **Validation step for the AI agent before building:**
  1. Browse TrustMRR categories and competitor pricing.
  2. Check Etsy and Google search suggestions for transactional "tool" queries.
  3. Build only where paying competitors exist but the long-tail variants are underserved.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue or demand data surfaced for kids' storybooks, pet portraits, custom songs, SEO audits, subtitle translation or resume tailoring (search budget exhausted).
- No keyword-volume or CPC data was gathered for these niches.
- I could not check whether any licensed music-generation API (e.g., one with explicit commercial rights) is suitable for an automated song service.

## 3. Which customer-acquisition channels can be automated, and how well do they work in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
The most automatable *owned* channel is **genuinely useful free-tool pages (SEO) plus one-time AI-directory listings**. It is slow and weakened by AI Overviews and by Google's scaled-content-abuse enforcement. **Marketplace discovery** (Etsy, Gumroad Discover) brings buyers without SEO, but the platforms' rules demand human-like responsiveness. **Paid ads** are automatable to run but uneconomic at $5–$20 price points. Automated outreach is spam and eliminated.

### Cited Findings
- **Seer Interactive study on AI Overviews:**
  - Organic CTR for informational queries showing Google AI Overviews fell **61%** since mid-2024; paid CTR on the same queries fell **68%**.
  - On queries *without* AI Overviews, organic CTR still fell **41%**.
  - Source: [Search Engine Land (Seer Interactive study)](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-drive-drop-organic-paid-ctr-464212)
- **Size and trend of the AIO effect:**
  - Estimates range from **−15.5%** (Amsive, 700K keywords) to **−61%** (Seer).
  - Pew Research's behavioural study of 68,879 real searches found users clicked a traditional result **8%** of the time when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% without one.
  - Secondary sources report organic CTR on AIO queries went from 1.76% to 0.61% (June 2024 to Sept 2025), then "rebounded to 2.4% by February 2026". Treat that rebound figure cautiously: it exceeds the 2024 baseline and I could not check it against Seer's original.
  - Sources: [Contently](https://contently.com/2026/04/27/ai-overview-traffic-impact/); [Omnibound](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/ai-seo-statistics); [SEO Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-ai-overviews-updates-2026-en/)
- **Google's scaled-content enforcement:**
  - Google made algorithm updates in January 2025 that penalise **scaled content abuse**. — [Beyond Indigo Pets](https://www.beyondindigopets.com/blog/how-google-is-cracking-down-on-scaled-content-abuse/)
  - Manual actions reportedly deindexed "hundreds of sites, many built on unreviewed AI content". — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-google-march-update-ai-pages-decimated)
  - "Programmatic SEO is not banned. Programmatic thin content is." A small site with a few dozen thin templated pages can violate the policy. — [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/scaled-content-abuse); [Meeeters](https://www.meeeters.com/seo-automation/scaled-content-abuse)
  - In the March 2026 core update, "unedited AI at scale lost 40–90% of its traffic" while "edited, first-party AI content gained 30–80%". — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-google-march-update-ai-pages-decimated) (SEO-agency blog, not Google data)
- **AI directories:**
  - "There's An AI For That" (TAAFT) paid listing starts at **$49**; "most founders opt for the **$347** one-time priority launch". It also sells PPC ads and **$99/mo** highlighted sidebars. — [TAAFT Get Featured](https://theresanaiforthat.com/get-featured/); [TAAFT Launch](https://theresanaiforthat.com/launch/); [AI Central Resources](https://www.aicentralresources.com/best-ai-tool-directories-submission-fees-comparison-list)
  - TAAFT is estimated at ~**785k** monthly visits and ranked the top AI directory by traffic in a "traffic-audited" list of 27 directories. — [zPlatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-directories/) (third-party estimate; may be inaccurate)
- **Product Hunt:**
  - Launches produce spikes of 5,000–50,000 visits; top-5 products get 10,000–30,000 on launch day.
  - Expect **10–30 signups per 1,000 visitors** (1–3%).
  - 50% of founders see some increase in registrations, 30% a significant one, and 16% none.
  - In an OpenHunts 2024 survey, 89% of Product Hunt founders said they would not launch there again.
  - Sources: [Shno Product Hunt statistics](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/product-hunt-launch-statistics); [Syften](https://syften.com/blog/product-hunt-launch/). These are aggregators, and OpenHunts is a competing launch platform, so the survey may be biased and possibly outdated.
- **Paid ads (vendor benchmark data, B2B-oriented):**
  - Average B2B SaaS CPC rose 29% year on year to **$8.86**; non-branded B2B SaaS CPC averages **$5.34**; first-touch ROAS is **78%**, below breakeven.
  - Average SaaS CAC is **$702** in 2026, and SMB-focused SaaS CAC runs **$200–$800**.
  - Source: [Ryze AI benchmarks](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/saas-google-ads-cost-benchmarks-budget-2026)
  - Counter-example: an indie reached $6,772 MRR with $20/day Meta ads. — [MRR Story](https://www.mrrstory.com/stories/this-indie-hacker-reached-6772-mrr-with-just-20day-meta-ads)
- **Marketplace discovery:**
  - Gumroad charges **30%** on sales through its Discover marketplace, versus 10% + $0.50 on direct sales. — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing); [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/tools/gumroad-fee-calculator)
  - Etsy's own search supplies buyers, subject to its AI-disclosure rules (§5). — [Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004)

### Inferences
Channels ranked by automatability × expected effect for this user:

1. **Free-tool SEO pages (programmatic, but each page is a working tool)** — automation 4–5/5.
   - The AI agent can build and maintain them.
   - The Seer and Pew data is about *informational* queries. Transactional "do X to my file" queries plausibly lose fewer clicks to AI Overviews, because a summary cannot perform the task. This is an inference; no dataset was found.
   - Risk: thin template pages invite scaled-content-abuse penalties (Jan 2025 and March 2026 enforcement).
2. **AI directories** — automation 4/5 as a one-time task. The agent can prepare submissions; some forms may need a human for captcha or account creation. Expect a small one-off spike plus backlinks, not steady income. Paying $347 for a TAAFT priority launch is hard to justify against a $50/month target (calc: it equals about 7 months of target profit).
3. **Product Hunt** — automation 2/5. It needs a human maker account and launch-day presence, and conversion is 1–3%. It is a one-time spike, so use it at most once.
4. **Marketplace discovery (Etsy, Gumroad Discover)** — demand arrives without SEO, but fees are higher (Discover takes 30%) and marketplace conduct rules require responsive communication (§5–6).
5. **Paid ads** — automation 4/5 to run, but economics fail at low price points.
   - Illustrative calc (assumptions: $1 CPC, 2% visitor-to-purchase conversion): CAC is about $50 per buyer, while a $12 product nets about $10.50.
   - At the vendor's $5.34 non-branded B2B CPC, CAC would be about $267.
   - Ads only work with subscription retention and constant creative testing, which is human work. The $20/day Meta example spends about $600/month, twelve times the target.
6. **Automated cold email or DMs, fake reviews, sock-puppet social posting** — ELIMINATE (spam and ToS violations; see §7).

### Gaps
- No consumer-side CPC or CPA data for AI tools. The ad figures are B2B-SaaS vendor benchmarks, and the Meta-ads CPA benchmark search failed because the budget ran out.
- No data on referral conversion from AI directories, or on how many visits a TAAFT free or paid listing actually produces.
- No data on AI Overview prevalence for transactional or tool queries specifically.
- Rules for automated posting on Reddit, X or other social platforms were not researched.

## 4. Unit economics: model/API cost per job, payment fees, refunds/chargebacks, fraud, support load

### Takeaway
For **text-output** products, model cost is negligible: about **$0.015 per job** on Claude Haiku 4.5, half that via the Batch API. Economics are dominated by **fixed per-transaction fees** (5% + $0.50 at most MoRs) and by **chargebacks**: one $15 dispute fee plus the lost sale wipes out roughly three $9 sales. Prices of **$12–$29** keep fee drag under ~10% and cut the sales needed for $50 net to **2–5 per month**.

### Cited Findings
- **Anthropic API prices** (per million tokens, input/output):
  - Claude Haiku 4.5: **$1 / $5**
  - Claude Sonnet 5: **$2 / $10**
  - Claude Sonnet 4.6: $3 / $15
  - Claude Opus 5: $5 / $25
  - The Message Batches API runs asynchronously at **50% of the cost**.
  - Sources: [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Batch processing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing) (via the bundled claude-api skill table, cached 2026-06-24)
- **Image generation API prices:**
  - Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image): **$0.039/image**; Nano Banana Pro Edit on fal: **$0.15/edit**. — [fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/nano-banana)
  - Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image): about **$0.067 per 1K image**; Lite version $0.0336. — [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-3.1-flash-image); [CometAPI](https://www.cometapi.com/ai-image-api-pricing/)
  - Nano Banana Pro: $0.134 per 1K/2K image and **$0.24 per 4K image**. — [GlobalGPT](https://www.glbgpt.com/hub/how-much-is-nano-banana-pro/)
  - Imagen 4: $0.02–$0.06 per image. — [Laozhang blog](https://blog.laozhang.ai/en/posts/gemini-image-api-guide-2026)
- **Merchant-of-record and marketplace fees:**
  - **Lemon Squeezy, Paddle and Stripe Managed Payments:** 5% + $0.50 per transaction. — [Comecero](https://comecero.com/blog/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy); [Lemon Squeezy 2026 update](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/2026-update); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/lemon-squeezy-stripe-acquisition-saas-founders-2026/)
  - **Polar (2026 restructure):**
    - The free **Starter plan is 5% + 50¢**.
    - Paid plans lower the rate: Pro $20/mo, Growth $100/mo, Scale $400/mo.
    - Organisations created **before May 27, 2026** keep an "Early Member" rate of 4% + $0.40 plus 0.5% on subscriptions.
    - International cards add **1.5%**; each chargeback costs **$15**.
    - Sources: [Polar fees docs](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees); [Polar Plans blog](https://polar.sh/blog/introducing-polar-plans); [Fungies Polar review](https://fungies.io/polar-sh-review-2026/); [Dodo Payments Polar review](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/polar-sh-review) (competitor)
  - **Gumroad:**
    - Direct sales cost **10% + $0.50**, with card processing listed separately at 2.9% + $0.30, for an effective **~12.9% + $0.80**.
    - Discover marketplace sales cost 30% (processing included).
    - Gumroad became a **Merchant of Record on January 1, 2025**.
    - Sources: [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing); [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/tools/gumroad-fee-calculator); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/gumroad-fees-explained)
  - **Dodo Payments:** 4% + 40¢ headline, plus 1.5% for international and 0.5% for subscriptions, for an effective ~6.5%. It says payments work across 220+ countries and territories. — [Dodo pricing](https://dodopayments.com/pricing); [Fungies comparison](https://fungies.io/polar-sh-vs-creem-vs-dodo-payments-mor-2026/)
  - **Creem:** 3.9% + $0.40, plus surcharges: splits 2%, affiliates 2%, cart recovery 5%, and **international payouts $7 or 1%**. It supports merchants in 86 countries. — [Dodo's Creem review](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/creem-io-review) (competitor-authored); [Creem supported countries](https://docs.creem.io/merchant-of-record/supported-countries)
  - **Etsy (US):**
    - $0.20 listing fee, charged again each time an item sells or auto-renews.
    - **6.5% transaction fee**.
    - Payment processing of **3% + $0.25** in the US; 4% + £0.20 in the UK; 3% + C$0.25 in Canada.
    - A **$15–$29 setup fee**, plus a "Regulatory Operating Fee" in some countries.
    - Sources: [Craftybase](https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees); [Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained/); [DodgePrint (setup fee)](https://dodgeprint.com/blog/etsy-setup-fee-how-much-to-launch-a-shop-on-etsy); [Listify](https://listify.studio/guides/etsy-listing-fees-2026-complete-guide)
  - **Fiverr:**
    - A flat **20% commission** on every order at all seller levels.
    - Withdrawal fees of **$0–$3**.
    - Funds are held **14 days** (7 for Top Rated, Seller Plus Premium and Pro sellers).
    - Sources: [VaultLeap](https://vaultleap.com/blog/fiverr-fees-explained-2026); [Fiverr Help — withdrawing earnings](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010530058-Withdrawing-your-earnings-managing-payout-methods); [Ruul](https://ruul.io/blog/fiverr-payments-guide)
  - **Upwork:**
    - A **variable 0–15%** freelancer fee since **May 1, 2025**, replacing the old 20/10/5% tiers.
    - Connects cost $0.15 each, and each proposal needs 6–16 Connects (**$0.90–$2.40**).
    - Freelancer Plus costs $19.99/mo.
    - Sources: [Upwork Help](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062538-Learn-about-the-Freelancer-Service-Fee); [GoLance](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026); [Vortenza](https://www.vortenza.com/guides/upwork-fees-2026)
- **Chargebacks and disputes:**
  - **Stripe:** a $15 dispute fee on every chargeback, non-refundable even if you win. Since **June 17, 2025**, contesting adds a **$15 "countered" fee**, refunded only if you win, so a lost contested dispute costs $30. Stripe's Smart Disputes waives the counter fee but takes 30% of any amount recovered. — [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/june-2025-pricing-updates-for-disputes); [Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/stripe-dispute-fees-2025)
  - **Lemon Squeezy** deducts the refunded amount (minus its platform fee) **plus a $15 dispute fee**. **Paddle's** agreement claws back the refunded amount **plus up to $20**. ## 5. Merchant-of-record and marketplace availability and policies (international sellers, AI-product acceptance, Lemon Squeezy after Stripe)

### Takeaway
MoRs broadly accept **self-serve AI software whose output is text or code**. They restrict **AI images or video of real people, face swaps, deepfakes, voice cloning, AI companions and adult content**, and they exclude **human services**. For a seller outside Stripe's 46 countries, **Lemon Squeezy** (still operating, with PayPal payouts to 200+ countries), **Paddle**, **Gumroad** (an MoR since 2025), **Dodo** and **Creem** are the options. **Stripe Managed Payments** (the product Lemon Squeezy is migrating toward) is excluded for businesses based in India, Brazil, South Korea and Turkey. Fiverr and Etsy allow AI-made work but require customisation or disclosure, which works against a zero-touch setup.

**Choosing a checkout by the user's country:**
1. **If the user is in one of Stripe's 46 countries and not in India, Brazil, South Korea or Turkey:** Stripe Managed Payments (5% + $0.50) is the most future-proof MoR, since Lemon Squeezy is converging into it. Polar Starter (5% + 50¢) is the developer-friendly alternative, but it adds 1.5% on international cards and its payout coverage is unverified.
2. **If the user is outside Stripe's countries:**
   - **Lemon Squeezy** is the practical default for global payouts (PayPal to 200+ countries). The catch is platform-transition risk while it migrates toward Managed Payments.
   - Paddle is the fallback. It has the strictest review.
   - Dodo claims 220+ countries.
   - Gumroad works everywhere it pays out, but at ~13% + $0.80 it is expensive at low price points.
   - Creem supports 86 countries, but its reported $7 international payout fee hurts at $50/month.

**Designing the product to pass MoR review:**
- Make it self-serve software with immediate access and text or file output ("AI as a feature").
- Avoid faces, voices, companions, adult content and music.
- Do not market it as a "done-for-you service". Polar and Managed Payments exclude human services, so describe it as automated software.

**Marketplace fit:**
- **Fiverr's rules are incompatible with a zero-touch pipeline.** Unmodified AI output falls below Fiverr's standard, clients can demand disclosure and non-AI work, and communication feeds the Success Score. Fiverr's own AI assistant was limited to Level 2+ and is being withdrawn.
- **Etsy is workable with honest disclosure.** Enforcement has tightened since June 2025, so the risk of listings being removed is material.

## 6. What ongoing work remains, and can an AI agent handle it?

### Takeaway
Most day-to-day work can be scripted or delegated to an AI agent: answering support email, auto-refunds, monitoring, content refresh, model migrations and spend caps. But **platform, policy and account events recur every few months** and need the human account holder. Examples: MoR pricing changes, a platform migration, discontinued features, updated terms, identity checks, and processor or domain reviews. Expect **roughly 15–60 minutes/month of unavoidable human attention** for an own-site tool (estimate), and substantially more on Fiverr or Etsy.

### Inferences
Residual tasks for an own-site AI micro-tool, and who can do them:

| Task | AI agent can do? | Human needed for |
|---|---|---|
| Support email (how-to questions, "didn't work", feature requests) | Yes. Triage and draft or send replies from a knowledge base; escalate edge cases | Rare escalations; anything requiring identity |
| Refunds | Yes, if a policy is encoded (e.g., auto-approve within 7 days via the MoR dashboard or API; the specific MoR refund APIs were not verified) | Disputes beyond policy |
| Chargebacks | Partly. Assemble evidence (logs, delivery proof) | Submitting evidence if the MoR requires the account holder; accepting losses |
| API key and billing | Yes. Monitor spend, enforce hard caps and rate limits, rotate keys | Funding or replacing the card on the API account; provider account reviews |
| Abuse and fraud (free-tier farming, card testing, prompt abuse, prohibited uploads) | Mostly. Rate limits, CAPTCHA, email verification, content filters, anomaly alerts | Deciding on bans or processor requests |
| Model deprecations | Yes. Watch provider deprecation notices, migrate model IDs, run regression tests | Approving the deploy if not fully automated |
| SEO and content upkeep | Yes. Refresh pages, fix errors, add genuinely useful tool variants | Strategy changes after an algorithm hit |
| Platform or policy changes (pricing, ToS, MoR migration) | Can summarise emails and diffs | Accepting new terms, re-KYC, migration decisions, tax forms |
| Payouts and taxes | No | Account holder only |

- **Why this beats marketplaces for zero-touch:** an own-site tool with an MoR concentrates human work into rare account-level events. Fiverr's per-order communication, revisions and Success Score, and Etsy's buyer messages about personalised files, create *per-sale* human work.
- **Main unquantified risk:** a single chargeback or a burst of free-tier abuse can erase a month's $50. Guardrails (auto-refunds, spend caps, rate limits) should be built on day one.

## 8. Top 1–2 survivors: concrete setup, exact current fees, and the evidence they can reach $50/month

### Takeaway
**Survivor #1 (KEEP, conditional):** a single "AI tool hub" website with 3–5 text or file-output micro-tools, a free rate-limited tier, and shared **credit packs at $12–$19** plus an optional **$5–$9/month** plan. Checkout runs through an MoR chosen by the user's country, and traffic comes from useful tool pages plus one-time directory listings. It needs only **3–5 sales/month** for $50 net. The realistic odds are **~15–30% by month 6**, not a sure thing. **Survivor #2 (MAYBE, weak):** an Etsy shop of AI-assisted, disclosed, original personalised digital items. It only makes sense if the user accepts a few minutes of message-checking a week.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences (setup plans)

**Survivor #1: "AI tool hub" (text or file-output micro-tools)**

*One-time human steps (about 2–4 hours; estimate)*
1. **Pick the MoR by country** (see §5):
   - In a Stripe-eligible country (not India, Brazil, South Korea or Turkey): Stripe Managed Payments, or Polar Starter.
   - Otherwise: Lemon Squeezy, or Paddle, Dodo or Creem.
   - Complete KYC and link the payout account. For a non-US seller on Lemon Squeezy, a bank payout avoids PayPal's 2%.
2. **Create an API account** with Anthropic (or another model provider). Add a card and set a **hard monthly spend cap** (e.g., $10).
3. **Register a domain**, and give the AI agent DNS and hosting access through API tokens.
4. **Approve the legal pages** the agent drafts (terms, privacy, refund policy) and submit the site for MoR review. Paddle may reject without reasons, so Lemon Squeezy, Polar or Stripe are the easier first choices.

*What the AI agent builds and runs*
- **Tools:** 3–5 narrow tools with transactional intent and a file or text deliverable. Candidates:
  - "Tailor my resume to this job ad" (PDF out)
  - "Translate this .srt subtitle file, keeping timings"
  - A niche generator for a specific document type
- **Free tier:** 1–2 free runs per day, protected by CAPTCHA and email verification to deter abuse.
- **Pricing:**
  - **Credit packs at $12–$19.** Net is $10.90–$17.55 after 5% + $0.50 (calc); API cost is about $0.30–$0.60 per 20 heavy jobs (calc).
  - An optional **$6–$9/month** unlimited-fair-use plan to build recurring income (10 subscribers at $6 ≈ $52 net).
- **Model routing:** default to Haiku 4.5; use Batch for anything non-interactive.
- **Guardrails:**
  - Automatic refunds within 7 days, to avoid $15+ dispute fees.
  - Per-IP and per-account rate limits.
  - Content filters, and no face or voice uploads.
  - Daily spend and error alerts.
- **SEO:** each page must be a working tool variant with unique examples. Target long-tail "do X to my file" queries. **No thin mass pages** (Jan 2025 and March 2026 enforcement). The agent refreshes content monthly.
- **Launch:** one-time submissions to free AI directories. The $49 TAAFT listing is optional; skip the $347 priority launch. At most one Product Hunt launch, which needs the human's maker account.
- **Maintenance cron jobs:** uptime checks, model-deprecation watch and migration, a weekly revenue and support digest emailed to the human, and a summary of any policy-change emails.

*Target math and consistency*
- **Target (calc):** 5 × $12 packs ≈ $51–$53 net after API cost, or 3 × $19 packs ≈ $52. With a 1% visitor-to-purchase rate (an assumption, not sourced), that means about 300–500 visitors a month to the tool pages.
- **Consistency:** credit packs are lumpy. Consistency comes from (a) several tools, (b) subscription conversions, and (c) evergreen transactional search demand.

*Why I believe it can reach $50 (and why it probably won't in one try)*
- **For:** verified-revenue data shows many tiny products at $50–$500/month (medians $145–$445; ~27% of revenue-positive projects under $100). One tiny AI tool is verified at exactly the target ($60/30 days). With $12–$19 prices, only 3–5 buyers a month are needed. Text-tool model cost is negligible, and MoR acceptance is straightforward.
- **Against:** ≥54% of listed products make $0; clicks from informational search are down 15–61% because of AI Overviews; there is no audience to launch to. Hence the 15–30% estimate by month 6 for a portfolio.

**Survivor #2: Etsy AI-assisted personalised digital items (MAYBE, weak)**

*Setup*
- The human opens the shop, pays the $15–$29 setup fee, verifies identity, and links payouts.
- The agent generates original designs and listing copy. Every listing gets an AI-disclosure line and "Designed by" attribution.

*Automation*
- **Preferred:** instant-download products (non-personalised, or personalised through a self-serve link). Etsy's policy on off-Etsy generator links is unverified, so confirm it first.
- **Otherwise:** personalised files must be sent through Etsy messages by the human, costing about 1–3 minutes per order (estimate).

*Economics and odds*
- A $9 item nets about $7.70 in the US before setup, ads and regulatory fees (calc). About 7 sales/month are needed for $50.
- Odds: 5–12% by month 3 and 10–20% by month 6 (judgement). There is no earnings evidence from this session, and enforcement is tightening.

*Why it survives at all*
- It is the only ToS-compliant option in this category with **built-in buyer demand** that needs no SEO or ads.
- **Drop it if the user refuses any weekly human touch.**
